Why Not Just Link Your Bank?

Bank-linked apps like Rocket Money scan your transaction history to detect subscriptions. This works, but it requires you to give a third-party app read access to every purchase, payment, and transfer you've ever made. That's a significant privacy trade-off — and unnecessary, since most subscriptions send a receipt to your email anyway.

Email-based detection finds the same subscriptions with a much smaller data footprint. You're sharing email receipts — not your entire financial life.

Method 2: Manual Email Search — Free, Takes 20–30 Minutes

2

Search Your Inbox Manually

Manual · Free

If you'd rather not use any app at all, you can find most subscriptions by searching your email inbox directly. This takes longer but requires zero account linking.

Step 1: Search for these keywords in Gmail or your email app

receipt invoice billing subscription renewal renews on thank you for your purchase your payment charged order confirmation free trial billed annually
  1. 1

    Search each keyword above in your primary email account. In Gmail, use the search bar; in iCloud, use the search field in Mail.

  2. 2

    Filter results by date (last 12 months) to catch annual renewals.

  3. 3

    For each result, note the service name, amount, and billing frequency (monthly or annual).

  4. 4

    Check the "Promotions" and "Updates" tabs in Gmail — subscription receipts often land there.

  5. 5

    Add what you find to a manual tracker like Bobby or a Google Sheets spreadsheet.

Tip: Also check your spam folder — some subscription receipts get filtered there, especially from less-known services.

Method 3: Review Credit Card or Bank Statements — Most Complete

3

Review Transactions Manually

Manual · No App Needed

For subscriptions that don't send email receipts (or send them to an address you rarely check), reviewing your credit card or bank statement directly is the most reliable manual method. You're logging in yourself — you're not giving an app access.

  1. 1

    Log in to your credit card or bank account website directly (not through any app).

  2. 2

    Download or view the last 3 months of transactions. Look at all months — annual subs may not appear in just one month.

  3. 3

    Sort by amount or merchant name to spot recurring charges. Focus on amounts in the $1–$30/month range.

  4. 4

    Flag any charge you don't immediately recognize — these are often forgotten trial conversions.

  5. 5

    For any charge you want to cancel but can't identify: use CentryAI's Cancel Finder — paste the merchant name and it finds the exact cancellation page.

Watch for: Small amounts ($1.99–$4.99) that repeat monthly — these are easy to overlook but add up. Also check for "ANNUAL" in charge descriptions which indicate once-a-year billing.
